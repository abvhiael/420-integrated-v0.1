# BET-AUDIT-5A.4 Workstream 2 — ciphertext availability and provider independence

## Enforcement

A shuffle stage is rejected unless the authenticated initial deck is certified, both operators have valid independently reviewed controller attestations, both complete 52-card ciphertext lists are available and match the canonical MiMC commitment on-chain, and the actual generated Reencrypt52 Groth16 verifier accepts the hand-, stage-, root-, threshold-key-, and transcript-bound proof.

The PokerShuffleCiphertextAvailability420 contract publishes all 208 affine field coordinates for each 52-card encrypted deck in both calldata and CiphertextDeckPublished logs, computes the same 52-of-64 padded six-level domain-separated MiMC Merkle tree as the gnark circuit, and rejects noncanonical inputs. Its 110 MiMC round constants are pinned to the actual gnark-crypto-derived Keccak digest 0x0ccfd8dfcda1a0713073bdce261f0321511e4d8f43d479899d398f64123ed3f8. The gate checks both roots against a recorded publication before calling the genuine proof verifier. Provider B's input must be the previously accepted Provider A output.

The ciphertext coordinates are public, but plaintext card values, provider permutations, and rerandomization witnesses are never emitted. Public archival indexing and redundant full node access are required for usable ciphertext availability after chain-history pruning. Missing history is a live-system DA incident even when a historical publication was once valid.

## Sole governance, independent evidence and no outside veto

420 Integrated's sole governance address is the ONLY address that may approve, renew or revoke provider eligibility. No auditor, reviewer or service provider can approve operators, revoke eligibility or transfer governance.

Each provider MUST originate its own on-chain, domain-separated claim, committing to its controller identity, evidence hash, expiry and replay-protected nonce, under its own distinct signing authority. An external auditor (neither provider nor governance) MUST independently originate an on-chain review record binding the same provider, controller identity and evidence hash to a content-addressed signed technical report and report location. Governance validates that externally retrievable technical evidence, selects the immutable audit ID to rely upon, then approves only a matching live claim and audit. Approval without a matching independently originated report fails closed. Each provider has a distinct controlling-group identifier, and both claims and approvals must be unexpired and unrevoked before either shuffle stage can proceed.

The registry exposes a permissionless append-only `recordAudit` pathway. Any independent technical auditor can publish a content-addressed signed report hash and location hash. The on-chain author is authenticated through the transaction signature and must be distinct from governance and both providers when eligibility is evaluated. Records are immutable and independently checkable. An auditor gains no approval, veto, revocation, administrator or governance role; governance may select a different independently produced report, but may not approve in the absence of at least one valid report. The report must be independently retrieved and cryptographically hashed. Its publisher's chain-signed audit submission is authenticated on-chain; any separate signatures embedded in the report must be verified off-chain. The contract establishes submission provenance and evidence commitment, not whether the auditor's off-chain findings are true.

For real providers, governance examines verifiable beneficial ownership and corporate relationships, independent key custody, signer privileges, separate machines/HSMs, independent randomness generation, control-plane separation, operator conflicts and common administrators. Auditors may present competing findings and emergency disclosures publicly; sole governance decides on eligibility and carries responsibility for acting on verified evidence. Different addresses or technical signatures alone never prove there is no hidden common controller or off-chain collusion. Synthetic tests do not certify production identities.

## Honest-provider and collusion assumptions

Privacy requires **at least one independently controlled honest provider** whose private permutation and encryption randomness are not disclosed to the other. If both providers collude, they may reconstruct the final permutation despite perfectly valid Groth16 proofs. A sole-governance approval and two provider-origin transaction signatures prove approval and signing-key custody, not that real-world controllers are independent or incorruptible. Residual risks include hidden shared ownership, coerced disclosures, shared HSMs, side channels, off-chain collusion, malicious auditors, chain data retention failures, governance capture, and compromised trusted setup.

Eligibility is re-evaluated at both shuffle stages. If sole governance revokes either provider, or either provider's independently attested claim expires or changes after stage one, stage two fails closed and existing deterministic hand recovery and provider accountability processes must resolve the stalled hand. No unreviewed replacement operator, bypassed verifier, or replayed proof is permissible.
