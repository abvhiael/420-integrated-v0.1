## 1. Ownership and canonical objects

The offer registry owns `ComputeOffer`, the request registry owns `ComputeRequest`, and the match registry owns the immutable accepted `ComputeMatch`; neither UI nor scheduler owns the authoritative versions. `offerId`, `requestId`, `matchId`, and `jobId` are distinct nonzero bytes32 IDs in separate chain-scoped namespaces, never recycled. Registry allocation must be collision-resistant, single-use and reconstruction-safe; the exact ID encoding and checked-in vectors remain an implementation gate, not an assumed executable mechanism. Every event identifies chain, registry/version, record ID, revision, actor and predecessor commitment where applicable. Mutable display metadata, transport routes, external market rankings and cache entries are non-authoritative.

**Offer** minimum canonical fields: `offerId`, authorized `providerId`, optional advertised `resourceId` or a **bounded class profile**, compute class/runtime and capability profile versions, service-unit/capacity definition and remaining reservable quantity, pricing policy ID/version and immutable formula/quote-parameter commitment, min/max per-job and concurrency constraints, availability start/end, region/privacy policy commitments, verification-compatible profiles, SLA policy ID/version, `expiresAt`, `revision` and `ACTIVE | PAUSED | CANCELLED | EXPIRED` state. Do not permit unbounded free-text prices, floating-point settlement, ambiguous unit conversions or a hidden provider-specific billing formula. An offer references the actual CMP-0.5 active provider and corresponding policy/security eligibility; a class-based offer has no accepted resource until a single eligible concrete node/resource is atomically resolved at matching. Different hardware listings can share an offer only when all accepted capacity and policy constraints remain explicit and objectively checkable.

**Request** minimum canonical fields: `requestId`, authenticated requester/payer and separately verified scoped delegates, CMP-0.4 `manifestHash` and accepted manifest authorization evidence, workload/resource/runtime requirements, input commitment, output/privacy/region/data-access constraints, partition/replica plan, verification profile ID/version and policy commitment, SLA/deadline, price/billing constraints, `maximumAuthorizedSpend` denominated in native $420 at Genesis, asset/payer-bound funding reference, creation/expiry, revision and `OPEN | CANCELLED | EXPIRED | MATCHED` status. `requestId` and `manifestHash` are bound by the requester's authorization, not inferred from a scheduler's payload. A request can exist before match; match/provider/resource/beneficiary must not be invented at creation. A signature on a manifest is not authority to debit a wallet, raise a budget, relax matching constraints or transfer custody. Any budget increase requires a separate requester-authorized funding action under CMP-0.8.

**Accepted match** minimum canonical fields: `matchId`, exact `requestId` and `offerId` with accepted versions/commitments, concrete `providerId`, `resourceId` and derived immutable-parent `nodeId`, accepted provider/node/resource revisions and eligibility snapshots, accepted manifest and work-plan commitments, accepted pricing formula/policy/version and fixed maximum/quoted total, service-unit and bounded metering terms, SLA/verification/privacy/region policy hashes, `acceptedAt`, policy-bound execution deadlines, capacity reservation reference, canonical provider-derived beneficiary/settlement reference, requester/payer/funding reference, and authorization evidence for both sides. The match is write-once after successful atomic acceptance. A separate canonical `jobId` may be linked under the CMP-0.2 authorized job/match binding; do not silently equate match and job IDs or create a second writable source for their economics.

## 2. Revision, authorization and publication

Offer creation/revision/cancel requires the provider's `offer.publish/update/cancel` capability scoped to `providerId` and to the underlying resource or class; a node liveness signature cannot set prices or redirect payout. Requests require requester or narrowly scoped `request.create/update/cancel` authority for the precise request and a separately bounded funding capability; provider keys never authorize payer funds. Match acceptance must prove **both** the requester-side authorized selection/constraints (direct selection or preauthorized bounded open-market selection) and the provider-side offer commitment/acceptance under the applicable published policy. An off-chain scheduler can recommend or relay a match but cannot accept on behalf of either party without a valid object-scoped grant. Direct matchmaking checks the exact requested offer/resource; open-market matching checks all published constraints and a requester-approved selection rule. No hidden matcher or admin override can broaden either side's constraints.

An offer revision updates only future eligibility. A materially changed price, resource class, capacity definition, privacy, verification, SLA, window or beneficiary policy invalidates outstanding unaccepted quote assumptions unless the requester explicitly authorizes the revised offer. A request revision changing manifest, spend, deadline, verification or constraints invalidates unaccepted match proposals and requires reauthorization as prescribed by the request policy. Existing accepted matches never inherit later revisions; preserve reconstructable snapshots and canonical revision events. Revocation/cancellation prevents **new** acceptance while preserving prior commitments and legitimate earned entitlements. Expiry is checked against canonical verification time at the *moment of acceptance*, not just at off-chain quote generation; expired offers/requests cannot be revived by replay or stale cache.

## 3. Atomic eligibility and match construction

At proposal time and again atomically at acceptance, fetch authoritative offer/request/provider/node/resource/policy/manifest/funding state and reject unsupported or stale versions. Check both parties' signatures/grants, chain and verifier domains, nonces, expiry, availability window, minimum/maximum job size, capacity, operational ACTIVE ancestry, AVAILABLE concrete resource, endpoint freshness, security/stake policy and required objective hardware evidence. Resolve class-based offers to exactly one eligible resource of the offered provider **before** match commitment; verify its immutable node/provider parent and runtime/capability revisions. Check that resource/region/privacy/verification policy, data-access permission, partition/replication and deadline meet *all* request and offer bounds. The accepted manifest and CMP-0.3 partition plan must match byte-for-byte; scheduling may not change executable, input, verification or resource requirements.

Compute a deterministic, integer-only accepted maximum from the bound pricing policy and quoted work plan. For fixed-price work, use the exact accepted fixed amount. For metered work, bind rounding direction, service-unit scale, maximum billable units, rate, timeout and any minimum/maximum fee so an independent client can reproduce the ceiling without external mutable rates; freeze the price-feed value/version if one is permitted by the policy. Quote the **aggregate** across partitionCount × replicationFactor, authorized retries/overheads, protocol fees and all providers/hops (if applicable), and prove checked multiplication/addition never exceeds `maximumAuthorizedSpend` or the actual funded reserve. A retry cannot create a fresh payable unit, reprice the match or increase the ceiling. No speculative overbooking: atomically reserve the exact resource capacity and required payer-bound Vault funding/obligation before a paid job may be ACCEPTED/RUNNING under CMP-0.7/0.8. Mere `FUNDED` bookkeeping is not proof of Vault custody. If any eligibility, reservation, nonce-consumption or funding check fails, revert the entire acceptance with no match ID, partial reserve or consumed authorization; concurrent acceptances must not oversubscribe capacity or double-spend the same request/offer budget.

On success, emit reconstructable `MatchAccepted` evidence with the exact canonical IDs, versions and economic/profile commitments; bind the selected provider-derived beneficiary and payer refund reference once. One request may be matched only as allowed by its explicit bounded multi-match policy; the V1 default is **one accepted match per request**. Multiple requests against an offer are allowed only while individual and aggregate capacity constraints hold. A partial-fill or multi-provider workflow requires a separately specified versioned policy allocating non-overlapping units, budgets, capacity and beneficiaries **before acceptance**; it is not enabled by a scheduler splitting a request informally. Cancellation/expiration races and duplicate message delivery are idempotent: a repeat of an identical accepted operation returns the same canonical result where policy permits, never a second economic commitment.

## 4. Frozen economics, execution and exceptional paths

The accepted match fixes provider/resource identity and revisions, beneficiary entitlement basis, unit price/ceiling, verification profile, deadline, manifest and work plan. No admin, scheduler, endpoint/key rotation or later provider-registration update can redirect that entitlement, expand acceptable compute class or move a node to another provider. Attempt-specific reassignment within the accepted provider is allowed only under CMP-0.5's **explicitly preaccepted** equivalence/recovery policy; a material provider/resource change requires a separately requester-authorized new match/job or rigorously specified recovery preserving prior obligations and at-most-once unit payment. Accepted execution remains subject to CMP-0.7 lifecycle checks; the match does not itself verify output, release Vault funds or guarantee a worker ran.

Offer pause, provider suspension, endpoint expiry and emergency pause stop new matching and unsafe new assignments but never confiscate already-earned verified claims or redirect payer refunds. An accepted job may enter explicit fail/dispute/refund routes on subsequent provider ineligibility; it must not silently reopen terminal states or erase signed history. Release an offer capacity reservation exactly once upon its authorized completion/cancel/fail/expiry path, with replay-safe accounting. `AssetVault420.cancelObligation` only restores the vault's free balance; actual payer withdrawal/refund must remain payer-bound and is specified/tested in CMP-0.8. No matching event alone unlocks a payout. Off-chain matching is replaceable, cannot block consensus/finality, and must not expose plaintext inputs, private location or bearer credentials in canonical state.

## 5. Implementation and executable qualification gate

Proposed code ownership follows frozen architecture: `ComputeOfferRegistry420.sol` validates provider capacity and versioned offer revisions; `ComputeRequestRegistry420.sol` validates requester-authorized manifest/constraints and funding references; `ComputeMatch420.sol` atomically checks both sides' live eligibility, concrete resource ancestry, pricing, capacity, authorizations, reservation and immutable acceptance. They call, rather than copy and independently mutate, CMP-0.5 registries, the CMP-0.4 manifest authorization verifier, CMP-0.2 job registry and an explicitly authorized CMP-0.8 Vault adapter. Proposed paths are **not claimed to exist as executable files**. Registry-resolved deployment and authorized code-hash publication must precede any active entry.
