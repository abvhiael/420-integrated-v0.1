# CMP-1.1 — verifier lifecycle and dispute release policy

Decision date: 2026-09-24. Scope: pre-testnet acceptance on draft PR #370. This is a release policy and explicit implementation boundary, not proof that dispute, reauthorization, settlement or refund machinery exists. No paid-job activation or payment authorization is permitted by this record.

## 1. Time boundaries and late verification

- `authorizationExpiry` in a signed request bounds **admission and continued use of the signed funding mandate**. `deadline` bounds ordinary execution admission. Neither timestamp is an implied authorization to increase the payer's spending ceiling or to create a different job. A submitted verdict has its own signed `expiry` and a separately expiring job/profile/controller-specific appointment; all must be live at submission.
- **Pre-testnet decision: fail closed on late verification.** After the signed request's `authorizationExpiry`, an unverified `RESULT_COMMITTED` job cannot accept a new verifier verdict under CMP-1.1. Do not bypass `fundingTerms()`, extend an expired signature, use a stale appointment, or treat an independently signed verdict as renewed payer consent. The existing `ComputeJobPolicyEnforcedVerification420.submitVerdict()` calls `fundingTerms()` and already fails closed after expiry. The registry deliberately allows a result already committed before the job deadline to be verified after that deadline **only while the request authorization, verdict signature, appointment and other admission requirements remain live**.
- A result already recorded as `VERIFIED` or `FAILED` before expiry remains an auditable historical decision; later expiry does not erase its record. The expiry itself does not release custody, trigger refund or extend a verdict's validity.
- An expired, unresolved job requires a **separately qualified terminal custody/dispute or refund process** before its reserve can be moved. If work is to be retried, obtain a **new owner/payer-signed request and independently funded job** after the original reserve is safely reconciled. Do not reuse the old request ID, release the payer's reserve on an operator assertion, or pretend CMP-1.1 currently has a working timeout/refund transition. Until CMP-1.2 qualifies this escape path, a funded job that may outlive authorization must remain test-only; release automation must deny it rather than risk stranded real funds.

## 2. Recorded verdict versus currently actionable approval

- An accepted verdict and its immutable `decisionRef`, signer, result commitment, appointment/evidence reference and recorded job transition are **historical provenance**: subsequent profile or identity changes do not rewrite what was recorded. `VERIFIED` in the job registry is not an irrevocable entitlement to settlement. A rejection recorded as `FAILED` is likewise historical, not an automatic refund instruction.
- **Profile approval revoked after recording:** the decision remains in history, but is **not currently actionable as an approved verification for any subsequent settlement** while its profile is disabled. The present `ComputeJobIndependentVerification420.verified()` intentionally checks live `approvedProfile` and returns false after revocation; the existing job status does not roll back automatically. Reapproval alone must not unlock an already challenged or tainted case: settlement eligibility requires an explicit later disposition and fresh controls. Profile revocation before verdict blocks acceptance outright.
- **Identity/controller attestation withdrawn, expired or materially corrected after recording:** preserve the historical decision, but **suspend its operational acceptance** pending an independent review of the four job-party controllers, appointment independence and the effective time/reason for the identity change. Withdrawal before verdict denies appointment eligibility. Do not infer that the current `verified()` view implements post-verdict identity suspension: it does not; downstream settlement must consult the current policy state and a qualified dispute hold or fail closed. Governance rotation also blocks new appointments under stale authority epochs, not rewriting history.
- Keep three concepts separate in clients and future contracts: `(a)` existence of a historical decision, `(b)` current operational eligibility, and `(c)` final economic disposition. A raw `Job.status == VERIFIED`, `decisionForJob != 0` or a historical `VerificationRecorded` event **must never alone authorize payout**.

## 3. Challenge, recusal, appeal and finality

- **Challenge grounds:** demonstrably wrong deterministic output/profile or evidence commitment; missing, expired, misattributed or conflicting identity/controller attestation; unqualified, economically interested or self-appointed verifier; incorrect match, worker or payer association; compromised key, signature or capability; unavailable evidence or an appointment obtained using materially false conflict disclosures. Preserve privacy: publish only opaque evidence commitments and reason codes, with sensitive controller material restricted to an independent reviewing authority.
- **Before a verdict:** a reported conflict or material challenge requires the selector/governance to revoke the job appointment or suspend the implicated principal/profile under the existing policy where authorized. The conflicted verifier must recuse; it cannot choose its replacement or review its own conflict. Only the independent selector may make a **new, separately evidenced appointment**, subject to the original request-authorization and verdict deadlines. No new verdict is accepted while the challenge is unresolved. The current contract checks revocation/suspension during submission but **does not provide a native challenge filing or pause switch**; the operational rule is enforceable only where an actual revocation/suspension transaction has been applied.
- **After a verdict:** a challenge **does not delete, rewrite or flip** the existing `decisionRef` or silently reset its used nonce. Record the challenged status and a separately signed disposition referencing the original job, decision, challenged evidence, reviewer/controller-independence rationale and chronology. The existing one-decision-per-job guard forbids in-place replacement. No automatic settlement, refund or second verdict is authorized during an unresolved challenge; a future custody/dispute mechanism must enforce a hold, and a future replacement-verification route must be separately specified, tested and authorized before use.
- **Appeals:** an appeal may review procedural independence, evidence integrity and deterministic recomputation by a newly independent reviewer who shares no conflicted controller with the original verifier or job parties. The reviewer cannot be the original selector acting alone or the challenged verifier. An appeal may produce `upheld`, `overturned` or `inconclusive` disposition evidence; it cannot itself transfer funds or mutate the immutable original decision. Only separately qualified settlement/refund and, if needed, replacement-job machinery can implement the economic consequences. Repeated or unsupported appeals must not make custody movable by default.
- **Finality:** CMP-1.1 supplies **record finality**, not dispute or economic finality. An unresolved challenge or identity/profile safety hold is a hard **no-settlement** condition. No party can declare an appeal resolved through an undocumented off-chain override. Define challenge filing authority, evidence-retention period, independent review quorum, response window, appeal window and executable on-chain hold/disposition interface as explicit deployment gates before any paid jobs or automatic settlement. No window or default outcome is invented by this pre-testnet record.

## 4. Mapping to current implementation and deferred enforcement

| Rule | Present enforcement | Additional gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New verdict after signed request authorization expiry denied | `ComputeJobPolicyEnforcedVerification420.submitVerdict` obtains payer via `ComputeJobSignedRequestAuthority420.fundingTerms`, which reverts after expiry | Timeout/terminal reserve path in CMP-1.2; do not activate funded live jobs before qualification |
| Verdict after execution deadline but within live signed mandate | Registry `_guard` permits `RESULT_COMMITTED` after job deadline; other submission/appointment/signature checks still apply | Check deployment-specific timing policy and all identities |
| New verdict under revoked profile, controller or appointment denied | Profile check in verifier; `ComputeVerifierIndependencePolicy420.eligible` at submission | Real-world beneficial-controller review and governance separation |
| Historical decision retained after revocation | Decision mapping and job transitions are immutable under current paths | Preserve evidence custody and reason/epoch provenance |
| Profile-revoked old decision non-actionable | `verified` checks live profile; recorded job status remains unchanged | Settlement must check contemporary eligibility, not only registry status |
| Post-verdict identity revocation, challenge, recusal/appeal hold | **Not yet implemented end-to-end**. Identity eligibility is checked at new submission, not in `verified`; registry has no challenge/appeal transition | CMP-1.2 dispute/hold/refund integration and qualified independent process; deny paid settlement until present |

## Qualification boundary

These decisions **resolve the written pre-testnet lifecycle policy**, not the outstanding executable dispute or custody gates. They do not qualify paid compute, release/refund, delayed verdict recovery, revised verdicts, review-body independence or production identity evidence. Before marking CMP-1.1 a full release candidate, reconcile this policy against all client/contract consumers and ensure no subsystem pays on a stale `VERIFIED` status; integrate executable holds and terminal reserve handling in their separately scoped stages. Requalify the exact final commit after any contract changes and after reconciliation with `main`.
