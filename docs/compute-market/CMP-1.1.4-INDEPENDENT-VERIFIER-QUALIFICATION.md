# CMP-1.1.4 — independently selected verifier and decision provenance

Status: IMPLEMENTATION IN PROGRESS on draft PR #370. The old signed-verdict adapter's earlier green CI is not sufficient to qualify the new independence policy. Do not merge, authorize paid-job activation or release funds on this basis.

## Governance and selection policy

`ComputeVerifierIndependencePolicy420` separates **governance**, **identity attestor** and **verifier selector**. Governance appoints/rotates these two operational authorities and can suspend an account and revoke a job appointment. The identity attestor must review verifiable off-chain evidence of the actual natural person, business group or ultimate controller behind each principal (including account delegates and smart-account signing arrangements). It records an opaque nonzero controller ID, an evidence hash, a finite expiry and an active flag for each relevant address. The evidence itself must be retained in access-controlled off-chain storage; never put identifying documents on-chain. A shared controller ID must be used for aliases, parent/subsidiary accounts and common-control arrangements, not one independent ID per wallet. Expired, withdrawn, missing and changed controller attestations fail closed; governance can suspend compromised accounts. This registry records an attestor's assertion; it cannot independently discover hidden beneficial ownership or guarantee a dishonest attestor will not lie.

**Eligibility:** a selected verifier must hold a current reviewed controller attestation, an approved workload-verifier profile and an actual job-scoped shared-registry `ACTION_VERIFY_RESULT` capability. The job owner, actual signed-request payer and matched resource operator must also have current controller attestations. Verifier controller must differ from each of their controllers; direct address equality is additionally rejected. The attestor must examine economic interest, common ownership/control, employment, affiliates and delegated signing; disclose conflicts and reject parties with a material stake in the outcome. These off-chain checks must be documented in the selection evidence. Independent third-party audit, eligibility/challenge procedures, conflicted-attestor recusal and verification-method qualification remain deployment requirements.

**Selection:** only a separately designated `verifierSelector` can appoint a verifier for a specific job/profile after assessing the actual job parties and recording a nonzero hash of a signed, independently reviewed selection and conflict-of-interest record. Appointments bind the job ID, selected verifier, approved profile, all four reviewed controller IDs, expiry and policy-authority epoch. Any missed attestation, conflict, missing appointment, altered job party/controller, suspension, revocation, expired appointment or governance authority rotation denies a verdict. The selector cannot grant verification capability or approve a verifier profile, and the verifier cannot appoint itself. Governance must independently control the selector and the attestor; a single person controlling all keys defeats the organizational separation despite separate wallet addresses. The current protocol does not itself enforce a multi-party governance quorum.

`ComputeJobPolicyEnforcedVerification420` is the **required production `ComputeJobRegistry420.verificationEvidence` endpoint**. It uses the canonical job owner and accepted-match resource operator plus the payer obtained from the canonical signed-request authority, NOT payer/operator values supplied by a verdict relayer. Its immutable policy contract must approve the specific appointment before it calls the signed-verdict implementation. The original `ComputeJobIndependentVerification420` is retained for legacy unit fixtures ONLY and must not be configured as the production verifier evidence endpoint. This policy gate applies to both approved and rejected verdicts; it runs before nonce consumption and any job state transition. Neither a valid signature nor an appointment proves numerical correctness or authorizes settlement.

## Tests and qualification evidence

`contracts/test/ComputeVerifierIndependencePolicy420.t.sol` covers distinct controllers and wallet aliases, absent attestations, unauthorized selection/attestation/revocation, changed or withdrawn identity, appointment expiry, governance suspension and authority rotation. `contracts/test/ComputeJobPolicyEnforcedVerification420.t.sol` wires the production adapter to the real `CapabilityRegistry420`, signed request, actual payer reservation, accepted resource match and worker receipt, and checks that a valid signature and capability fail without an appointment or when the verifier shares the owner's controller. It checks authorized independent appointment succeeds without releasing payer funds, and appointment revocation prevents a verdict and nonce consumption. Existing canonical registrar, grant expiry/revocation and profile-authority tests remain in `ComputeJobCanonicalVerifierAuthorities420.t.sol`.

**CI evidence:** new policy contract, production adapter and their tests require fresh Solidity Contracts, 420 Integrated Qualification and 420Docs success on the exact final SHA. Record run IDs and resolve failures before qualifying this sub-gate. Passing local test actors does not attest target-chain control, real beneficial-ownership evidence, verifier correctness or production governance. Record deployment-specific registrar, issuer, selector, attestor, governance and profile-authority addresses/code hashes and real policy evidence separately.

## Remaining full-1.1.4 gates

Define public conflict criteria, challenge/appeal and recusal, evidence custody, privacy, controller-identity reconciliation and governance oversight. Complete adversarial cross-job nonce, revoked capability/profile, resource drift, ERC-1271, signature malleability and reentrancy tests, and decide historical verdict validity when approval or a controller attestation is revoked. The signed-request authority's `fundingTerms` currently requires a live authorization expiry; the production policy-enforced verifier therefore fails closed after that expiry and a late-verification rule must be specified. Current `verified()` checks live `approvedProfile`, so revocation can invalidate previously recorded evidence; qualify its intended lifecycle before deployment. Reconcile against current `main` and rerun all required workflows on the exact merge candidate. CMP-1.2 controls payment release and refund, CMP-1.3 hardware/metering and CMP-1.4 the broader verifier registry; no correctness or payment claim may rest on a signed verdict alone.
