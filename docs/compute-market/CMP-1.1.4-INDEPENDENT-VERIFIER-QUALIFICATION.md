# CMP-1.1.4 — independently selected verifier and decision provenance

Status: pre-testnet implementation and qualification on draft PR #370. The signed-verdict adapter's green CI alone does not qualify live verifier independence, paid-job activation, disputes or fund release.

## Governance and selection policy

`ComputeVerifierIndependencePolicy420` separates **governance**, **identity attestor** and **verifier selector**. Governance appoints/rotates these two operational authorities and can suspend an account and revoke a job appointment. The identity attestor must review verifiable off-chain evidence of the actual natural person, business group or ultimate controller behind each principal (including account delegates and smart-account signing arrangements). It records an opaque nonzero controller ID, an evidence hash, a finite expiry and an active flag for each relevant address. The evidence itself must be retained in access-controlled off-chain storage; never put identifying documents on-chain. A shared controller ID must be used for aliases, parent/subsidiary accounts and common-control arrangements, not one independent ID per wallet. Expired, withdrawn, missing and changed controller attestations fail closed; governance can suspend compromised accounts. This registry records an attestor's assertion; it cannot independently discover hidden beneficial ownership or guarantee a dishonest attestor will not lie.

**Eligibility:** a selected verifier must hold a current reviewed controller attestation, an approved workload-verifier profile and an actual job-scoped shared-registry `ACTION_VERIFY_RESULT` capability. The job owner, actual signed-request payer and matched resource operator must also have current controller attestations. Verifier controller must differ from each of their controllers; direct address equality is additionally rejected. The attestor must examine economic interest, common ownership/control, employment, affiliates and delegated signing; disclose conflicts and reject parties with a material stake in the outcome. These off-chain checks must be documented in the selection evidence. Independent third-party audit, eligibility/challenge procedures, conflicted-attestor recusal and verification-method qualification remain deployment requirements.

**Selection:** only a separately designated `verifierSelector` can appoint a verifier for a specific job/profile after assessing the actual job parties and recording a nonzero hash of a signed, independently reviewed selection and conflict-of-interest record. Appointments bind the job ID, selected verifier, approved profile, all four reviewed controller IDs, expiry and policy-authority epoch. Any missed attestation, conflict, missing appointment, altered job party/controller, suspension, revocation, expired appointment or governance authority rotation denies a new verdict. The selector cannot grant verification capability or approve a verifier profile, and the verifier cannot appoint itself. Governance must independently control the selector and the attestor; a single person controlling all keys defeats the organizational separation despite separate wallet addresses. The current protocol does not itself enforce a multi-party governance quorum.

`ComputeJobPolicyEnforcedVerification420` is the **required production `ComputeJobRegistry420.verificationEvidence` endpoint**. It uses the canonical job owner and accepted-match resource operator plus the payer obtained from the canonical signed-request authority, NOT payer/operator values supplied by a verdict relayer. Its immutable policy contract must approve the specific appointment before it calls the signed-verdict implementation. The original `ComputeJobIndependentVerification420` is retained for legacy unit fixtures ONLY and must not be configured as the production verifier evidence endpoint. This policy gate applies to both approved and rejected verdicts; it runs before nonce consumption and any job state transition. Neither a valid signature nor an appointment proves numerical correctness or authorizes settlement. Production deployment must use the profile-evaluating subclass and canonical wiring qualified in 1.1.4.3–1.1.4.4.

It checks authorized independent appointment succeeds without releasing payer funds, and appointment revocation prevents a verdict and nonce consumption. Existing canonical registrar, grant expiry/revocation and profile-authority tests remain in `ComputeJobCanonicalVerifierAuthorities420.t.sol`.

**CI evidence:** policy contract, production adapter, profile evidence and wiring tests require Solidity Contracts, 420 Integrated Qualification and 420Docs success on the exact final SHA. The pre-testnet integrated acceptance record is `CMP-1.1.4.5-INTEGRATED-PRETESTNET-CLOSEOUT.md`. Passing local test actors does not attest target-chain control, real beneficial-ownership evidence, verifier correctness or production governance. Record deployment-specific registrar, issuer, selector, attestor, governance and profile-authority addresses/code hashes and real policy evidence separately.

## Lifecycle and dispute decisions — resolved in writing, not implemented as paid-job controls

**Authoritative release policy:** [`CMP-1.1-VERIFIER-LIFECYCLE-RELEASE-POLICY.md`](CMP-1.1-VERIFIER-LIFECYCLE-RELEASE-POLICY.md) specifies signed-request expiry/late verification, historical provenance versus currently actionable verdicts, profile and identity revocation, pre-verdict recusal, post-verdict challenges, independent appeals and settlement holds. In brief: no late verdict after request authorization expiry; a historical decision is not erased by subsequent revocation; current profile revocation makes an old approval non-actionable; an identity/conflict challenge must suspend payout pending independent disposition; challenged or appealed decisions cannot be rewritten, re-signed over or paid/refunded by CMP-1.1 alone.

**Critical implementation gap:** the existing `verified()` performs a live profile-approval check but does not dynamically validate post-verdict controller attestations or provide a challenge/appeal hold. A registry status of `VERIFIED` is immutable historical provenance and is NOT a settlement entitlement. The signed-request expiry gate can leave an already funded and result-committed job without a qualified terminal/refund path. Therefore the written policy is settled for the pre-testnet design baseline, but paid-job release is prohibited until executable dispute holds, identity rechecks and terminal custody/reconciliation are qualified in the applicable subsequent scope. Do not label the current implementation as a complete dispute/appeal system.
