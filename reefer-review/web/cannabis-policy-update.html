Cannabis policy update: Ottawa seeks input on tracking-rule changes

As of October 8, 2026: Canada's cannabis regulatory framework is still evolving, with a new federal consultation on cannabis tracking requirements and continuing updates to guidance for licensed businesses.

A consultation, not an enacted rule

Health Canada announced a consultation on proposed amendments to the Cannabis Tracking System Order on September 29, 2026. The proposed changes seek to streamline reporting and reduce regulatory burden while maintaining government oversight. The proposal was published in the Canada Gazette, Part I, on September 26. The federal comment deadline is November 10, 2026, at 11:59 p.m. Eastern time. A proposal is not the same thing as a rule that has come into force.

What is already in effect?

Separate cannabis regulatory streamlining amendments took effect March 12, 2025. Those reforms covered licensing, physical and personnel security, production, packaging and labeling, and reporting. Businesses should not confuse that completed 2025 round of changes with the new 2026 tracking proposal.

What operators should be watching

Federal guidance updates in 2026 have also addressed cannabis regulatory fees, monthly tracking reports, pesticide testing expectations, odour controls and products containing intoxicating cannabinoids other than delta-9 THC. The practical question for licensed operators is which requirements already apply, which guidance clarifies existing rules and which proposals remain open for public input.

Why this matters

Reporting and compliance obligations have real operational costs, particularly for smaller producers. Changes designed to simplify reporting deserve close attention, but they must be evaluated alongside traceability and public-health oversight. For readers, the clearest way to follow the issue is to distinguish a proposed regulatory amendment from a published final rule, and to check the effective date before acting.
